INTRODUCTION
Background to the Problem
Bangladesh has a large and growing FMCG sector. Companies like Pran, Akij, Square, and Bashundhara distribute products across the country through dealer networks. Currently, the dealership acquisition process is entirely manual — prospective dealers must physically visit company offices, submit paper documents, and make payments in person.
Local shopkeepers have no efficient way to find the nearest authorized dealer or compare discount offers. They rely entirely on word-of-mouth and personal contacts, causing supply delays and missed business opportunities.
The root cause of the problem is the absence of a centralized digital platform connecting FMCG companies, dealers, and shopkeepers. This leads to slow onboarding, poor visibility in the distribution network, and inefficient communication throughout the supply chain.
Solution to the Problem
The proposed solution is the Online FMCG Dealership Management System — a web-based platform where prospective dealers can apply digitally, upload documents, and pay fees online. Administrators approve applications from a centralized dashboard. Shopkeepers find the nearest authorized dealer using geolocation, compare discount offers, place orders, and submit expired product return requests.
This solution is appropriate because it uses standard web technologies, requires no specialized hardware, and directly solves the manual inefficiency at every layer of the distribution chain.
Existing systems: ShopUp (Bangladesh) helps retailers with ordering and financing but does not handle dealer onboarding. Udaan (India) offers B2B distribution but is unavailable in Bangladesh. No existing platform provides online dealership application, document verification, expired product return management, and shopkeeper-facing dealer discovery for the Bangladesh FMCG market.

FEATURES NOT TO BE TESTED
The following areas will not be directly tested
:
Payment gateway integration (bKash, SSL Commerz) — actual transaction processing is the responsibility of the third-party provider. Payments are simulated/mocked during testing.
Third-party SMS and email notification delivery — only that the system triggers the correct event is verified; delivery by external providers is not tested.
Native mobile apps (iOS/Android) — web-only system; mobile apps are not in scope.
Database server administration and backup procedures — infrastructure concerns outside the application boundary.
Browser extensions and third-party plugins — behaviour when such tools are active is not tested.
These exclusions were made considering the limited timeline, resource availability, and the current feature priorities of the project.
TESTING APPROACH
Testing Levels
The testing for the Online FMCG Dealership project will consist of Unit, System/Integration (combined), and Acceptance test levels. Most testing will be done by the QA lead with the development team's participation due to the academic project timeline and team size.
Unit Testing
UNIT Testing will be done by the developer of each module and approved by the QA lead. Proof of unit testing (test case list, sample output, defect information) must be provided by the developer before the module is accepted for integration testing.
System/Integration Testing
SYSTEM/INTEGRATION Testing will be performed by the QA lead and the development team. All modules will be tested together to verify end-to-end workflows. A module may enter integration testing with up to two minor defects as long as they do not block testing of the core workflow.
Acceptance Testing
ACCEPTANCE Testing will be performed by the QA lead and frontend developer simulating real end-user workflows — dealer application, admin approval, shopkeeper search, order placement, expired product return — from start to finish.
Test Tools
Chrome DevTools — for inspecting UI, network requests, and console errors during manual testing.
Postman — for testing all backend API endpoints directly.
GitHub — for source version control and defect tracking.
Google Sheets or Notion — for logging test case results and bug reports.
All test cases in this plan are executed manually by the QA lead.
Meetings
The test team will meet once every day during the 10-day project window to evaluate progress and identify defects early. The QA lead will meet with the development team lead every two days. Additional emergency meetings may be called if a critical defect is found.

STAFFING AND TRAINING NEEDS
There is one full-time QA lead assigned for system/integration and acceptance testing phases. The two backend developers handle unit testing for their own modules and provide evidence to the QA lead. The following training areas are required:
Developers and QA tester need familiarity with Postman for API endpoint testing and Chrome DevTools for frontend debugging.
QA lead requires training on structured test case writing following the IEEE 829 format used in this document.
All team members need to understand the system's user roles (Dealer, Shopkeeper, Admin) and workflows to support acceptance testing accurately.
10.1 Staffing Requirements
The project team consists of four (4) members who collectively serve as developers, testers, and QA reviewers. Although this is an academic project, roles have been distributed to simulate a real industry environment:
QA Lead / Test Manager (1 member): Responsible for writing and executing the test plan, managing defect logs, and signing off on acceptance criteria. The QA lead coordinates all testing activities and escalates critical defects.
Backend Developers (2 members): Responsible for implementing the PHP MVC controllers, database queries, AJAX endpoints, and unit testing their own modules before handing off to the QA lead.
Frontend Developer / UI Tester (1 member): Responsible for CSS layout, template views, and browser-level UI verification. Also participates in acceptance testing by simulating real user workflows.
10.2 Training Requirements
To perform complete and proper testing, the following training areas must be addressed before the testing phase begins:
Technical Training — PHP MVC & AJAX: All team members require a working understanding of the Native PHP MVC routing structure (Controllers, Models, Views) and how AJAX requests are handled to correctly interpret test failures versus code bugs.
Tool Training — Selenium WebDriver with Python: The QA lead and supporting testers need hands-on training with Selenium for automated regression testing of login, search, and messaging workflows.
Tool Training — VS Code & Git Workflow: Developers must follow the agreed push/pull workflow using Git to ensure test environments are always in sync. Untested code branches must not be merged before QA sign-off.
Security Awareness — SQL Injection & XSS: All team members need basic awareness of common web vulnerabilities (SQL Injection, Cross-Site Scripting) to identify potential security defects during testing. Input validation and prepared statements must be verified in every module.
Admin Dashboard Training: The team member simulating Administrator role during acceptance testing must be trained on the full moderation workflow — User Request review, Product Request approval/rejection, and notification handling — to accurately execute acceptance test cases.

The CampusKart project follows a structured 6-month development and testing timeline from 27 April 2026 to 19 May 2026 (22 weeks). The schedule below outlines the major testing phases and milestones. This Gantt chart demonstrates how testing activities overlap with development to enable early defect detection and iterative quality improvement.

Phase 1 — Planning and Design Reviews (Week 1 (27 Apr – 03 May 2026)): System Design Review, Detail Design Review, Acceptance Criteria Definition, and Test Plan creation are completed. Test procedures and rules are finalized.
Phase 2 — Test Planning and Documentation (Week 1–2: 27 Apr – 10 May 2026): Unit Test Plans, System/Integration Test Plans, and Acceptance Test Plans are drafted in parallel with the development kick-off. All screen prototypes are reviewed and finalized by 07 May 2026. Report mock-ups reviewed and approved by 10 May 2026.
Phase 3 — Content Creation and Prototype Reviews (Week 1–2: 27 Apr – 10 May 2026): UI wireframes and screen prototypes for all test case modules are finalized. Report structure, mock-ups, and prototype reviews are completed. System interface documentation is submitted to team members for review.
Phase 4 — Development and Coding Phase (Week 1–2: 27 Apr – 10 May 2026): Core PHP MVC modules (Authentication, Marketplace, Messaging, Admin Dashboard) are built and verified. AJAX endpoints and MySQL database handshakes are continuously tested by the QA lead using Chrome DevTools and Postman. All modules are code-complete by 10 May 2026.
Phase 5 — Quality Assurance and Defect Management (Week 2–3: 04 May – 17 May 2026): Unit testing runs iteratively alongside development. System/Integration testing verifies all end-to-end workflows (Login → Listing → Messaging → Admin Moderation). Defect logging and triage tracked in Google Sheets. P0/P1 defects must be resolved before acceptance testing begins. DRE target: 95%.
Phase 6 — Acceptance Testing, Reporting, and Defense Preparation (11 May – 19 May 2026): UAT is conducted by the QA lead simulating real student and admin workflows. Regression testing runs until three consecutive clean passes are achieved. Final defect/incident reports are produced. Test logs and turnover documentation are completed. Go/No-Go deployment decision and project defense are on 19 May 2026.

Resource and Staffing Contingency
Critical Constraint: As of 19 May 2026 (Defense Day), the most significant operational risk is team availability during the final acceptance testing week. The project follows a structured No-Skip-No-Bypass policy — no testing phase will be omitted to save time. If a team member is unavailable, the QA lead will 
assume additional responsibilities and the final launch date will be extended by a maximum of 5 days to maintain 100% quality assurance coverage.
 
Risk Monitoring
The QA Lead will conduct a brief risk audit at every weekly team meeting. Risks are tracked using a shared issue tracker (GitHub Issues or Google Sheets). Any risk that escalates from Medium to High must be immediately reported to the full team and a revised mitigation plan documented within 24 hours. The Gantt chart in Section 12 will be updated in real-time to reflect any schedule slippage caused by materializing risks.
Team member unavailable during testing week: QA lead will extend working hours to cover execution. Developers must document unit test results in advance so QA lead can proceed independently.
Module not ready in time for integration testing: Available modules will be tested first. Incomplete modules marked as 'Blocked' in defect log and tested when available. No part of the testing process will be bypassed.
Unclear or changing requirements: Requirements are frozen at the SRS in this document. Any change requires agreement from all 4 members and must be documented before implementation.
Test environment unavailable: Localhost development environment will be used as fallback for all testing activities.
Too many defects found late in the cycle: Critical defects only are prioritised. Minor defects accepted with documentation if deadline cannot move.
